## Planning

### js-yaml Evaluation (GHSA-h67p-54hq-rp68) — LEAVE ALONE

**Decision: Do NOT add the `js-yaml>=4.2.0` override.**

Rationale:
- Currently installed: `js-yaml@3.14.2` via `@yarnpkg/parsers@3.0.2 -> js-yaml@^3.10.0`
- `@yarnpkg/parsers@3.0.2` declares `"js-yaml": "^3.10.0"` in its dependencies — this constrains js-yaml to the 3.x series
- The advisory `GHSA-h67p-54hq-rp68` specifies `vulnerable_versions: <=4.1.1`, `patched_versions: >=4.2.0`
- pnpm audit is numerically treating `3.14.2 <= 4.1.1` as vulnerable — this appears to be a registry misflagging of v3 as within the v4 advisory range
- Adding `"js-yaml>=4.2.0": ">=4.2.0"` as an override would force a MAJOR version jump (v3 → v4) into a package whose upstream requires `^3.10.0`, risking breakage of nx's `@yarnpkg/parsers` YAML parsing
- The advisory is moderate severity and currently BELOW the Layer 6 gate; risk of breaking nx tooling outweighs the benefit
- Correct mitigation path for this advisory requires `@yarnpkg/parsers` to upgrade its own js-yaml dependency (nx upstream concern); we cannot safely override it here

**This is NOT a suppression entry** (no `audit-suppressions.toml` change needed): the advisory is below the gate, and the reason not to fix it is breakage risk from a misflagged major-version boundary — not a deliberate acceptance of the vulnerability. If nx bumps `@yarnpkg/parsers` to one that depends on `js-yaml@^4.x`, the advisory will resolve naturally.

## Lessons Learned

1. **A pnpm override can clear advisories beyond the one it targets.** Bumping `vite` to `^6.4.3` cleared the high `server.fs.deny` advisory AND the moderate `launch-editor` NTLMv2 advisory in one move — both shared the `vite <=6.4.2` root. Always check whether a bump collapses multiple advisories before treating moderates as separate work.
2. **"Below the gate" is not "no record required."** A non-gating advisory with no safe upgrade still belongs in `audit-suppressions.toml` for traceability and noise-suppression — the async-std precedent already established this. The fix-now cost (one entry + regen) is far below the recurring re-derivation cost of an untracked, recurring audit line.
3. **Numeric advisory ranges can span major versions.** `js-yaml <=4.1.1` numerically includes the entire 3.x line, but the only patched release is in 4.x — so a 3.x consumer pinned by an upstream constraint has no in-range fix. Recognise this shape early; the disposition is suppress-and-track with an upstream-bump trigger, not a forced major jump.
